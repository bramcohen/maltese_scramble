# The solver

`ladder.py` solves a uniformly random state of the Maltese Gear Cube in a mean of **135 HTM** in about **35 s**
(134 HTM and 28 s with the optional large table), every solution verified. Inverting the solution of a uniformly
random state gives a uniformly random scramble (`scramble.py --random-state`). No solve failed in the benchmarks:
a rung that finds nothing is retried from other landings, and the whole solve restarts at worst. All costs are in
the half-turn metric (HTM: a single or double turn of a face is one move, a triple or quadruple turn two; adjacent turns of the
same face merge first).

## Vocabulary

- **Corners**: the 8 corner pieces, each with a position and a twist (3 orientations).
- **Faces and edges**: the 18 pieces that are neither corners nor gears, one piece type with 18 slots: 6 are the
  faces (one sticker; their rotation is invisible), 12 the edges (two stickers; their rotation, the *spin*, has 4
  values). Any of the 18 can sit in any slot. The code calls them ring pieces.
- **Gears**: the 24 gear pieces, each with a position and a spin (4 values). The 16 *top/bottom* gears sit around U
  and D when solved, the 8 *equatorial* gears between them. Each gear has a *sibling*, the other gear of its edge.
  The four gears of one colour look identical.
- **Moves**: `U`, `R`, `F` quarter turns (D, B, L are held still). `R4` is not the identity (the ring has turned
  180°). The 21 single-face *runs* `X, X2, X3, X4, X', X'2, X'3` are the solver's alphabet.
- **Group, subgroup, coset**: the states reachable from solved form the group (8.82e69 with one corner fixed). A
  subgroup is closed under some moves ("everything reachable with U, R2, F2"); the cosets of a subgroup T are the
  classes of states that differ by an element of T. A rung's *classes* are the cosets it must tell apart.
- **Invisible subgroup S**: the states that look solved — face rotations and permutations of same-coloured gears —
  1.96e11 of them (37.5 bits). Two states that differ by an element of S are the same scramble.

## The ladder

A Kociemba-style solver picks nested subgroups and solves one rung at a time, each rung with moves that keep the
previous rungs' work. Here there are eight rungs (rungs 1 and 2 are solved together):

    everything --1+2--> Z --3--> Y2 --4--> H1' --5--> H1 --6--> A1 --7--> F16 --8--> solved

| rung | lands in | what it fixes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 + 2 | Z | every corner twisted correctly; the 8 equatorial gears on equatorial slots, the 8 top/bottom edges on top/bottom edge slots; the spins of the top/bottom edges sum to an even number of quarter turns, as do the spins of the equatorial gears (the set without the two spin conditions is four times larger, and short sequences do not reach the rest of it) |
| 3 | Y2 | spins: the top/bottom edges and gears are right or off by exactly a half turn (an even number of edges), the equatorial gears share one spin; Y2 = Y and its invisible parity-swapped coset |
| 4 | H1′ | each top/bottom gear shares an edge with its sibling (which edge is still free) |
| 5 | H1 | the gears are where and how a solver using only U, R2, F2 could have left them: from here a gear's half-turn comes right by itself |
| 6 | A1 | the white and yellow gears all face up or down, so every top/bottom edge is an assembled block; a free choice of which corner pair the inner chain glues |
| 7 | F16 | the group generated by U, R4, F4 and eight relocated full turns: 393,216 states |
| 8 | solved | a finish table over F16 |

Why these subgroups and not others: because they are the ones with short generating sequences and class counts a
table can hold; the boundaries were found by measurement, not derived.

### A rung

Every rung except 6 and 8 is a meet in the middle over a precomputed table. The *backward table* holds, for every
class of the target's right cosets reachable within N HTM by sequences that stay in the rung's starting group, the
cheapest cost and a witness; the *forward list* holds every element of the starting group up to E HTM with its
moves. A probe composes the input with every forward element and looks the result's class up in the table (a
50-point canonical form hashed to 64 bits, `fulltab/ft.c`): a hit of forward cost e and table cost c is a solution
of e + c moves, its moves the forward element's followed by the inverse of the table representative's; the move
sequence is reconstructed and its landing verified by label before it is accepted. Rung 6's table is complete (every
class, a lookup); the finish is a table over all of F16.

**Up to relabelling.** The eye cannot see face rotations or which same-coloured gear is which, so a rung only has
to land in *some* invisible renaming's coset of its target. Renaming the pieces is left multiplication by an element
of S, so the rung renames its *input* every allowed way (the set is a transversal of S ∩ target in S ∩ starting
group, `fulltab/treps.py`) and probes all copies in one pass; the beam continues from the renamed landing, and the
whole solve ends in an invisible state rather than the identity. Renamings per rung: 1+2: 1,296 (which two of each
side colour's four gears count as equatorial), 3: its Y/Y2 parity bit is the target itself, 4: 9,216 (which U/D
gear is sibling to which side gear, over both cosets of Y2), 6: 16, 7: 256, 8: 4 (face rotations). Together they
are the 37.5 bits of S; the per-rung table below separates what a rung has to tell apart from what its labels distinguish.

### The beam

Each rung returns every landing within its *window* of its best, distinct by the next rung's class and ranked by the
merged length of the whole sequence so far; the next rung is run on the *keep* cheapest of them. Rungs 3 and 7 run
once per solve and are retried only when they find nothing (the previous rung's spare landings, then its wider
window, then a restart of rungs 1 + 2 with a wider window that skips landings already tried). Rungs 1 + 2 try the
three orientations of the fixed corner and, if all three miss, shoved-in first moves. Rung 6 tries 16 renamings × 8
inner frames (the rotations that keep H1), the finish its 4 renamings of every rung-7 landing; the cheapest merged
total wins. Defaults (`Ladder.PARAMS`): windows 1 / 2 / 2 / 6 / – / 6, keeps 1 / 8 / 16 / 100 / 1 / 50 after rungs
1+2 / 3 / 4 / 5 / 6 / 7.

*Positions to tell apart* is log₂ of the number of positions of the rung's starting set that look different modulo
its target: what the rung has to resolve, and the number the HTM-per-bit ratio uses. The implementation labels more
than that (*classes labelled*): coset labels also separate labellings the eye cannot see, the 37.5 bits of the
invisible subgroup S spread over the rungs. It makes up for it by probing every allowed renaming of the input in the
same pass, so those bits cost no HTM; they are an internal matter of table size and probe time.

*Failure rate* is how often the probe reaches nothing; *solutions found* is how many distinct landings one run
yields within the rung's window, which is what the beam chooses from. The floor for the last column is the move
alphabet: after the first run each run turns one of the two other axes, with four cost-1 and three cost-2 runs per
axis, so the number of words grows 8.69× per HTM, 3.12 bits per HTM, **0.32 HTM per bit**; up to 9 HTM the words are
nearly all distinct states. At that rate every visible state (194.9 bits) would be within 63 HTM.

(`RAW=1 fulltab/beam_full.py 24 6`):

and 3.3 / 4.6 / 4.5 / 3.7 / 6.8 / 5.0 / 0.1 s; with the default table rung 7 takes 12.7 s and gives 30.3 HTM.

## Building the tables

`PYTHONPATH=. .venv/bin/python fulltab/build.py` runs every step below in order, skipping the ones already on disk;
it builds the small rung-7 table (1.9 h). `--rung7 large` builds the large one instead (13 h, 1.1 TB of disk while
building), which saves 1.3 HTM and 7 s per solve; the solver uses the large table whenever it is present.
Everything derives from the puzzle model: the stabiliser chains of the subgroups (`tables/bsgs_*.pkl`, sympy, from
the generating words in `fulltab/mkbsgs.py`) and `fulltab/data/ball9.npy`, every state within 9 HTM of solved
(417,745,816 states, 42 GB, 1 minute). Nothing generated is checked in. The steps, from the repository root with
`PYTHONPATH=.`:

| step | command | output | time | memory |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| chains | `fulltab/mkbsgs.py` | tables/bsgs_Y, H1p, H1, A1, F16, Z, Y2.pkl and invisible_gens.pkl (generators of S); the generating words are documented there and checked against the 7-HTM ball by `fulltab/census.py` | 15 s | small |
| the ball | `fulltab/ball.py 9` (and `7`) | ball9.npy + parents | 1 min | 50 GB |
| finish | `fulltab/finish.py` | tables/finish_full.pkl.gz: rung 8's table, meet in the middle on the ball over all moves to 18 HTM | 8 min | 50 GB |
| pieces | `fulltab/pieces.py 5 3`, `6 4`, `7 5` | the rungs' excursions to 10, 12, 14 HTM (the chains' steps; also the tables' move reconstruction) | seconds | small |
| renamings | `fulltab/treps.py`, `treps2.py`, `treps_y2.py` | T_reps_12/6/7/8/4_y2.npy | 40 min (sympy) | small |
| rungs 1 + 2 | `fulltab/ztable.py 9` | full_rung12_b9.npy + witnesses | 2 min | 10 GB |
| rung 3 | `CHAIN_TARGET=Y2 fulltab/chain.py 3 9 10 20` | full_rung3_chain20y2 + reps (57.9M classes) | 4 min | 60 GB (the ball gathered to a disk file) |
| rung 4 | `fulltab/chain.py 4 9 12 28 30` | full_rung4_chain28 + reps (267.6M classes) | 10 min | 60 GB |
| rung 5 | `fulltab/chain.py 5 9 14 28 30` | full_rung5_chain28 + reps (131.9M classes) | 8 min | 60 GB |
| rung 7 (default) | `fulltab/slicebuild.py 7 10 64 1 30` | full_rung7_s10m (20 HTM, 364.8M classes, 8 GB) | 1.9 h | 50 GB |
| rung 7, large (optional) | `fulltab/slice2.py 7 9 128 1 29` then `fulltab/merge2.py 7 9 1 64` | full_rung7_t11m (21 HTM, 1.28e9 classes, memory-mapped files, 31 GB) + t11_7/P1,R1 | 3 h + 9 h + 1.3 h | 60 GB, 1.1 TB of disk while building |
| rung 6 | built on first use (coset.CostMITM, tables/cache/rung6) | 1.3M classes | 2 min | small |
| forward lists | built on first use | fulltab/data/mm/fwd_* | minutes each | 20 GB |

A rebuilt table has the same classes and costs but not necessarily the same representatives (the joins are
multi-threaded), so solutions on rebuilt tables are equivalent, not identical; a rebuilt chain can also label the
same cosets differently, so every table must be built from the chains it will run with: the 24 states of the regression run gave
134.75, 133.17, 134.38 and 135.04 on four builds of the same tables. `fulltab/slice2.py` and `merge2.py` resume from their files
on disk after an interruption. The builds were done on a 128 GB machine; the 50 to 60 GB steps fit a 64 GB machine
one job at a time with nothing else heavy running, under `fulltab/guard.sh PID 20`, which kills a job if the
machine's free memory drops below 20 GB. Validation:
`fulltab/witcheck.py 7 full_rung7_t11m 11 4000` decodes random witnesses of the large table, `fulltab/test_solve.py <rung>` solves
random inputs of a rung and verifies the moves. At run time the tables and forward lists are memory-mapped
(`fulltab/data/mm/`, 30 GB, 52 GB with the large rung-7 table); the ladder loads in about 10 s and holds about 20 GB
of its own memory.
